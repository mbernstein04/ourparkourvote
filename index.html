What the brief says

The signatures are valid. The law says let the people vote.

01

The people signed

The City Clerk certified both petitions — over 4,000 valid signatures each, well beyond what the Charter requires. The signatures are not in dispute.

02

The law favors a vote

For forty years, Minnesota courts have held that a certified measure goes to the voters unless it is manifestly invalid — invalid beyond serious dispute. A contested legal theory is not that.

03

Duluth has been here before

In 1972, when a Duluth council refused to let residents vote on a rezoning, the Minnesota Supreme Court ordered the election. Denney v. City of Duluth. The answer was to let the people decide.

04

Placing is not endorsing

Putting the measures on the ballot decides only who decides — it keeps the choice with the people of Duluth. We believe that when Duluthians get a voice, better things will happen.
